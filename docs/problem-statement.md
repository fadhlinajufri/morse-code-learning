## Problem Statement
People with severe motor disabilities such as Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS) often cannot write, speak or use their arms, so ordinary ways of communicating are no longer available to them (Pavithraa, 2026). Eye blinks usually stay controllable even in late-stage impairment, and when combined with Morse code, short blinks can stand for dots and long blinks for dashes. The problem is that this only works if the user has already learned the code, and beginners find it hard to memorise and tell apart so many dot-dash patterns (Umale, 2026). Most existing work focuses on detecting blinks and decoding them into text, with little support for teaching the code or checking that it has been learned. Without lessons and feedback, nobody can tell whether a user is ready to communicate by blinking.

In the case of the patient with severe motor disabilities such as those with Locked-In Syndrome or quadriplegics, before they can even start conveying information through effective communication means, the choices may already be severely limited (Kannan, A., Vinoj, A., Pathirikattil, A., Prasad, D. V., & Moozhippurath, B., 2026). For this reason, simple structure requires user to produce only two types of signals making it easier for individuals who have difficulty using conventional keyboards or other physical input devices.

## Users and Scope
Main users and stakeholders

Group	| Role

Individual with limited hand mobility	- Primary users who learn Morse code through blinks
Caregivers and family members	- Help set up the system and support practice
Therapists / healthcare professionals - May monitor progress and decide when a user is ready
Project supervisor and evaluators - Assess the system’s functionality and accuracy
Developer - Builds, tests and maintains the system

In scope
- Detecting short and long blinks with a standard webcam, facial landmarks and the Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR)
- Lessons that introduce a few randomly selected letters (A–Z) or numbers (0–9)
- Blink-based exercises with immediate feedback
- Evaluation

Out of scope
- Full text entry or general communication features
- Specialised hardware (eye trackers, EEG, wearable sensors)
- Symbols, punctuation and prosigns beyond A–Z and 0–9
- Clinical trials or medical-grade validation
